➡️ What exactly is the EU Kids Act?

The 'EU Kids Act' is a proposed law aiming to make the internet 'Child friendly'. The law suggests that this goal must be achieved via age-gating and moderating digital platforms by implementing governmental age checks with official identifying documents on the internet.

Without verification via official documents your access will be blocked or restricted in:

Social Media Online Gaming AI Chatbots

You can take a look at the full text of this proposed legislation on the European Union's official website for it.

➡️ How does this law affect us?

• It compromises our digital liberties

Having the government act as a guard at the gates of the internet could prove to be a slippery slope against free speech on social media and basic online privacy.

• It un-necessarily puts our identifying information at risk

Forcing citizens to link official government identification to internet services creates a massive target for cyberattacks, a vulnerability already demonstrated when security researchers successfully hacked the EU's 2 million € 'zero knowledge' age verification app twice within three months of its launch.

• It places an age-lock for everyone

Getting access to social media, buying a game on Steam or the Playstation & Xbox stores, playing online games, using an AI chatbot are all going to be either heavily restricted or blocked by default for anyone that isn't willing to hand over his ID.

➡️ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ's)

How does the email button work? +
It generates a pre-formatted email in your device's default mail app using a secure 'mailto:' link. We do not track or store any data.
How can I manually send the email? +
Select your country and write your name in the boxes above. Then scroll down to the 'Copy' section. Click copy on the To, Subject, and Body fields and paste them into Gmail, Outlook, or whatever mail provider you use!
Where can I learn more about this law? +
Check the links provided in the 'What exactly is the EU Kids Act?' section, and read coverage from digital rights organizations like the EFF and EDRi.
